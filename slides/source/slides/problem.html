01 · The problem

Brain surgeons use a map, and the map goes wrong.

Before surgery, the patient gets an MRI: a detailed 3D picture of their brain. During the operation, the surgeon uses that picture as a map to find the tumour.

But once the skull is opened, the brain shifts out of place. Now the map shows things in the wrong place.

A wrong map can mean leaving tumour behind or cutting healthy brain.

Rubric 01, Problem Definition. Who it affects: brain surgeons, their patients, and hospitals that can’t afford an MRI machine in the operating room (it costs millions).
