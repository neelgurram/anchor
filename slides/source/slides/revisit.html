Quantum ties today. Here’s what it needs.

Today a normal computer solves our puzzle in a thousandth of a second. Quantum gets the same answer, but not faster.

Real surgery needs 80–200 qubits. To get there, chips must make far fewer mistakes, and answer in under a second.

Exact needed rates: 9.6e-3, 3.9e-3, 1.2e-3, 3.9e-4. Other conditions: the puzzle must also beat RANSAC (needs rigid-fit terms); the computer must sit in or near the hospital (our cloud queue took hours); patient data must stay within privacy rules. Per try, QAOA hit the exact best answer far less often than a normal computer (0.07% vs 79% at 16 qubits), but keeping its best try still gave the same final map.
