SAY: I built it in Qiskit: a parameterized QAOA circuit, tuned in a hybrid loop with a normal computer, and run with the Sampler. Then we ran 48 puzzles on IBM’s real ibm_fez chip. In 42 of them the chip’s best answer was perfect, and the map was as good as a normal computer’s. It got 83 percent of the valid answers our noise model predicted, six times better than random guessing. So we compared it with a simulator, analysed the noise, and used error suppression. — IF ASKED: Rubric 03, technical names for judges: QAOAAnsatz parameterized circuits (p = 1–3); COBYLA optimizer on a CVaR objective (hybrid loop); LR-QAOA digitized annealing; preset transpiler level 3 onto IBM Heron; SamplerV2 primitive on Aer, the FakeMarrakesh noise model and ibm_fez; XY4 dynamical decoupling plus gate and measurement twirling. Built: the whole pipeline, API and 3D app. Reused: our own Q-Router QUBO helpers, RESECT patient data, a standard brain mesh. Hardest part: keeping hardware runs replayable from raw results. Hardware bonus, top tier (+5): ran on a real device AND compared to the simulator (blue = perfect simulation) AND analysed the noise AND applied error suppression. Same 24 circuits as our noise study, 6–12 qubits, three circuit lengths, sent once raw and once with error suppression (XY4 dynamical decoupling + twirling). Noise protection gave no gain this time (85% as many valid answers as without); the two jobs ran about three hours apart, so calibration drift is part of that. At 12 qubits the longest circuit kept 4–13% valid answers vs 22–33% for the shortest. In the 6 runs that missed, 5 maps came out worse. Two patient cases on one night is a small sample.
