The surgeon’s map is an MRI scan.

Before the operation, the patient gets an MRI: a detailed 3D scan of their brain.

During surgery, a navigation system shows the surgeon where their tools are on that MRI, so they can find the tumour.

The MRI is the surgeon’s map.

The MRI map. Purple dots are landmarks: spots that can be found again on later scans.

The pre-operative MRI is taken days before surgery. Surgical navigation (like a GPS for the surgeon’s tools) registers the patient’s head to that MRI at the start of the operation. Landmarks here are expert-marked points from the RESECT dataset. The 3D brain is a standard model brain used for display; all measurements come from the real patient data.
