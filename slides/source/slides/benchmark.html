Anchor’s map is over twice as accurate.

What the graph shows: each bar is one method; its length is how far off the fixed map is, on average, in millimetres. Shorter is better. The dashed lines mark no fix at all (right) and the best possible (left).

We tested on 22 real patients from RESECT (a public brain-surgery dataset), 264 times, and checked against expert-marked spots the computer never saw.

Trusting every clue: 5.6 mm off. Quantum: 2.2 mm, the same as the best normal-computer answer.
