Anchor’s map is over twice as accurate.

Trusting every match leaves the map 5.6 mm off, worse than not fixing it at all (5.4 mm), while every method that keeps only the matches that agree gets it to about 2 mm. Picking the right matches is what fixes the map, and quantum picks them as well as a normal computer does.

We tested on 22 real patients from RESECT, a public brain-surgery dataset. To grade each fix, we hid some landmarks and measured how far off the map put them.

Trusting every match: 5.6 mm off. Quantum: 2.2 mm, the same as a normal computer.

A standard method called RANSAC did slightly better (2.0 mm), and we report it.
