Finding that group is a quantum puzzle.

It’s like finding the biggest group of friends at a party where everyone knows everyone else.

Each match becomes a qubit: keep it or drop it. In superposition, the qubits hold every possible group at once. The circuit makes the best groups more likely, then we measure many times (sampling) and keep the best answer.

1 match = 1 qubit

Keeping a match earns a point; keeping two that disagree costs points.

for the demo patient: 6 landmarks, 2 possible matches each

Why quantum?

Puzzles like this get much harder as they grow. Today a normal computer is still faster; slide 14 says what would change that.

Scientists call this a maximum-clique problem, written as a QUBO

6 / 17

For judges: this is a maximum-clique problem written as a QUBO: minimise −Σxᵢ + P·Σxᵢxⱼ over pairs that disagree, with P = 2 so breaking a rule never pays. A test checks every small case by brute force to prove the best score is always the true biggest group. Max clique is NP-hard in general. Quantum ideas used: superposition (all selections at once), interference steered by the circuit settings, and sampling.
